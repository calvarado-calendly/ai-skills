# Review Patterns

## Manager Review Openings

Strong performer:

```text
I recommend Exceeds Expectations for [Name]. [They] delivered above-level impact through [core strengths] and trusted ownership of [specific work].
```

Short evidence window:

```text
I recommend Consistently Meets Expectations for [Name] based on the evidence available so far. [They] joined [team/scope] late in the cycle, so my direct observation window is shorter than it is for most people in this review period.
```

Constructive review:

```text
I recommend Meets Most Expectations for [Name]. [They] have the capability to contribute at a high level, but inconsistent [execution/follow-through/communication] has kept their impact below what I expect for [level/role].
```

Self-review:

```text
I recommend Exceeds Expectations for my [period] performance. This cycle, I operated above the expected [level] scope by [scope], [operating model], and [concrete outcomes].
```

## Strength Themes

Use two to four themes. Pick themes that match the evidence.

- Technical depth and implementation judgment
- Ownership through ambiguity
- Cross-functional coordination
- Platform or systems thinking
- Operational judgment and production readiness
- Mentorship and leverage through others
- Product and customer context
- Practical decision-making
- Communication that clarifies decisions, tradeoffs, and risk
- Leadership transition, delegation, and operating rhythm

Good strength paragraphs connect pattern, evidence, and impact:

```text
[Name]'s clearest strength is [theme]. That showed up in [project/work], where [they] [role/action]. The impact was [result/change/risk reduced/team enabled].
```

When current-level and next-level rubrics are available, make the alignment explicit:

```text
At [current level], this work shows [current-level expectation]. It also creates [next-level signal] because [scope/leverage/strategy/stakeholder impact], though I would separate that signal from a full next-level readiness claim unless the evidence is sustained.
```

Use notable work and accomplishments as the proof, not as a standalone list. Connect each major accomplishment to current-level expectations first, then to next-level signal when supported.

## Areas of Improvement Patterns

Strong performer:

```text
The main growth area for [Name] is scaling [their] impact through [delegation/operating systems/visibility/measurable outcomes]. [They are] already trusted for [strength], but at [level] the expectation is not only to solve the hardest problems. It is to make the strategy clear enough that others can execute with less dependence on [them].
```

Newer employee:

```text
The next growth area for [Name] is building more consistency in [technical autonomy/project ownership/domain judgment]. At [level], I expect [specific behavior]. The next step is [clear behavior] with [planning/dependency/risk/stakeholder] signal earlier in the work.
```

Emerging leader:

```text
The main growth area for [Name] is turning strong personal ownership into a more durable team operating model. [They have] often been the person who understands the requirements, knows the tradeoffs, and keeps the work moving. The next step is making that clarity repeatable through clearer owners, decision logs, written tradeoffs, and status updates.
```

Constructive:

```text
[Name] has the technical ability to be a strong contributor. The concern is inconsistent [execution/follow-through/communication]. Work has too often [evidence pattern]. At [level], I expect [specific next behavior].
```

## Evidence Boundaries

Use explicit boundaries when needed:

- `My direct management period ended on [date], so this review is focused primarily on the work I observed before that transition.`
- `Given [Name]'s short time on the team, my direct observation is still limited.`
- `I would keep the rating anchored to evidence through [date]. The later [event] is useful follow-through signal, but it falls outside the review window.`
- `The evidence supports [current-level rating]. I do not see enough sustained evidence yet for [next level], but I do see progress toward that direction.`

## Editing Pass

Remove or rewrite:

- Defensive counter-rating language, such as `I would not rate this as...`
- Generic praise, such as `goes above and beyond`, unless tied to specific work.
- Research-note residue, such as source lists, raw quote stacks, or issue inventories.
- Repetitive sentence frames across paragraphs.
- Overly abstract claims, such as `demonstrated leadership excellence`, without examples.

Prefer:

- `The evidence supports...`
- `That showed up in...`
- `The next step is...`
- `At [level], I expect...`
- `This matters because...`
